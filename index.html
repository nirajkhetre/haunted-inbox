Find every account you forgot you made.

You forgot them. They didn't forget you.

Haunted Inbox reads the senders and subject lines in your mail, on your own computer. It lists your old accounts, shows which were hacked after you joined, and takes you to where you can delete them.

  • Free and open source
  • No account or sign-up
  • Never opens your messages
  • Your mail stays on your computer

How it works

  1. Give it your mail. Drop in a Google Takeout file, or connect with an app password. It asks only for the sender, the date and the subject of each email.
  2. It finds your accounts. A small open AI model, Gemma, runs on your computer and reads one short summary per sender. Plain code then checks the dates against a public list of hacks.
  3. You close them, riskiest first. Each account links to where you can delete it. You mark it off when you're done, and you can undo that.

What it reads, and where it goes

Sender, date and subjectStay on your computer. A local model reads them.
The message itselfNever asked for and never read.
Your app passwordKept in memory for the scan, never saved.
A dropped mail exportDeleted as soon as its senders and subjects are read.
"Who owns it now?"Optional. Sends only the company's public name to a web search, and only when you press it.

Does it work?

Tested on one real inbox of 7,556 emails, with 166 senders marked by hand. The usual advice is to search your mail for "welcome" and "verify".

Questions

What does it cost?
Nothing. It's open source under the MIT license, and there's no account.
Will it delete my accounts for me?
No. It takes you to the right page. Closing the account is your decision, on their site.
If it says an account was hacked, was my data taken?
Not necessarily. It means the company was breached after you joined, according to the public Have I Been Pwned list.
Why is there a graveyard?
The report is for getting it done. The graveyard is the same accounts laid out by the year you joined, for when you'd like to see how far back it goes. It's optional.
Where does the data come from?
Hacks from Have I Been Pwned, delete links from JustDeleteMe, both bundled in the code. Made by Niraj Khetre.
